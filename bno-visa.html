The BN(O) visa, explained.

The BN(O) route lets British National (Overseas) status holders and their immediate family from Hong Kong live, work and study in the UK, and leads to settlement. Here is what you need to know, and how we can help.

Who is eligible?

To qualify, you must show UK Visas and Immigration that:

  • You are a British National (Overseas).
  • You are 18 or older.
  • Your permanent home is in Hong Kong if you apply from outside the UK; or in the UK, Channel Islands, Isle of Man or Hong Kong if you apply in the UK.
  • You can show enough money to support yourself and your family for 6 months in the UK, unless you have already lived in the UK for 12 months (not on a Youth Mobility Scheme visa).
  • You can provide a TB test certificate, unless you already provided one for an earlier UK visa.

Do I have BN(O) status?

BN(O) status was created on 1 July 1987 by the Hong Kong Act 1985 and the Hong Kong (British Nationality) Order 1986. If you registered as a BN(O) before 1 July 1997, or were under 18 then and were added to a parent’s passport, you can apply. If you are not sure, HM Passport Office can check.

How long is the visa?

You choose to apply for 30 months (2 years 6 months) or 5 years. The visa can be extended as many times as you need before you qualify for settlement.

Which family members can come with you?

Partner

Spouse, civil partner, or unmarried partner who has lived with the BN(O) status holder for 2 years or more.

Household child

Children or grandchildren under 18 of the BN(O) status holder or their partner.

Household member

Children aged 18 or over of the status holder or their partner, born on or after 1 July 1997, with their own partner and children who normally live with them.

Adult dependent relative

Parents, grandparents, brothers, sisters, sons or daughters (18+) who normally live with the status holder and are exceptionally dependent on their care.

Every dependant has to prove their relationship with the BN(O) status holder. We help you collect and check the documents for each person.

Meeting the financial requirement

There is no fixed amount. You need to show enough to support your family for at least 6 months in the UK. The Home Office uses this formula:

Settlement after five years

After 5 years in the UK, BN(O) visa holders can apply for settlement (indefinite leave to remain). The earliest you can apply is 28 days before you reach 5 years. Time on these visas before switching to BN(O) also counts: Global Talent (or Tier 1 Exceptional Talent), Investor, Entrepreneur, Skilled Worker (or Tier 2 General), Minister of Religion, Sportsperson, Representative of an Overseas Business and UK Ancestry. Time on a Student or Youth Mobility Scheme visa does not.

To qualify you need to:

  • meet the English requirement, with a qualification at B1 or above, or a degree taught in English (with an Ecctis statement if it was studied outside an English-speaking country);
  • pass the Life in the UK Test;
  • have spent no more than 180 days outside the UK in any 12 months during the 5 years;
  • not have breached immigration rules.

Settlement rules are under review by the government. We will tell you if anything changes for your family.

Frequently asked questions

I don’t have enough funds. Can I rely on a third party?

Yes. You can rely on funds or support from a third party, for example a credible offer from relatives or friends in the UK to share accommodation with you, as long as it does not breach the overcrowding rules.

Can the lead applicant and family members apply separately?

A partner and children under 18 can apply later to join the status holder. A grandchild or adult dependent relative must apply together with the status holder as the same household, within 48 hours of the lead application. An adult child of a status holder (born on or after 1 July 1997) can apply independently, whether or not their parent has applied.

Can we travel to the UK separately?

Children under 18 must travel with one or both parents unless they are joining parents already in the UK. Other family members can travel separately.

I am already in the UK. Do I still need to show 6 months of funds?

Not if you have lived in the UK on another route, such as a student or work visa (but not the Youth Mobility Scheme), for 12 months before you apply.

I can’t travel within 90 days of my vignette date. What now?

You can apply for a replacement vignette using the Home Office online form.

I don’t live in Hong Kong or the UK now. Can I still apply?

You can apply from any country, for example while studying or working abroad, but you must show your ordinary residence is in Hong Kong. That is unlikely if you have permanent or settled status in another country.

Can I withdraw my application?

Yes, before a decision is made. The Immigration Health Surcharge is refunded; whether the application fee is refunded depends on how far the application has progressed.

My entry clearance application was refused. What can I do?

There is no right of appeal against an entry clearance refusal, but if the Home Office made an error you can apply for an administrative review. The application fee is not refunded; the Immigration Health Surcharge is.
